Chairman's Verdict

Where the Council Agrees high confidence

  • Go 100% local for the MVP. Unanimous. Sensitive screen data (NSFW/games) on a "cheap VPS" is a breach headline and an install-killer. Per-item local-vs-VPS choice is premature and offloads a trust decision users won't make.
  • Cut Hermes Agent from the MVP. 4 of 5 call it over-engineering — a self-improving agent to answer "how productive was I" is "a spaceship to cross the street." Self-improvement matters at month 6, once the core loop is proven.
  • Accountability is the memorable hook. A tracker alone is a commodity graveyard (RescueTime, Toggl, Rize). The character with an opinion is what people screenshot and share.
  • Smallest slice = local sample → classify → one honest call-out. Test whether you keep it running two weeks without muting it.

Where the Council Clashes real disagreement

  • Wedge order. Contrarian: the tracker gets the install; personality is earned after the data is trusted. Everyone else: the accountability call-out IS the product and the reason to install. Both agree you build the same data pipe first — they disagree on whether v0 should talk yet.
  • Is the honest log an asset or a liability? Expansionist wants to sell "reality reports" from it. Every peer review flagged this as the plan's biggest contradiction — monetizing NSFW logs one breath after "your shame stays on your machine."

Blind Spots the Peer Review Caught emergent

  • Classification accuracy is THE core risk — and nobody sized it. A vision model reliably telling "work YouTube" from "slacking YouTube," or research from doom-scrolling, is unsolved. Every call-out and every "productive hours" number rides on it.
  • A tough-love buddy is MORE fragile to being wrong than a dashboard. A dashboard that miscategorizes is mildly annoying; a buddy that calls you a bitch for doing research gets uninstalled instantly. So "lead with accountability" and "the tracker must be accurate first" are the same requirement.
  • Nobody defined "productive." There's no ground truth — the entire premise assumes a number that may not exist without the user setting the rules.
  • Consent & legality of always-on screen capture. On-screen third parties (client messages), work-laptop policy, platform ToS, app-store rejection risk. Continuous capture itself may be the disqualifier, regardless of where the bytes land.
  • Cost & retention unmodeled. Per-frame vision API spend at always-on cadence adds up; and does anyone voluntarily keep a device that insults them past week two?

The Recommendation do this

  • Build the "accuracy-first" local MVP — with the personality present but gentle. The Contrarian is right that accuracy is the gate, but the First Principles Thinker is right that the risky, unproven thing is the emotional loop. Reconcile them: ship a 100% local tool with no Hermes, no VPS, no voice, no calendar, that samples the screen, classifies into a few coarse buckets, and delivers one honest daily call-out through the cute character — with a tone dial (gentle → savage) so a wrong call-out at low intensity doesn't nuke trust.
  • Instrument it to measure its own accuracy: let yourself thumbs-down a wrong call-out. That correction log is both your accuracy metric and, later, the honest seed for personalization.
  • Defer Hermes/VPS/cloud/benchmarking until two things are true: the classifier is right often enough to be trusted, and the daily call-out actually changes your behavior for two weeks.
The one thing to do first

Build the dumbest local loop this week — screen sample every 30–60s → one cheap vision call → coarse category → local SQLite → one honest sentence per day from the buddy. Run it on yourself for a week and track two numbers: does the sentence make you flinch, and how often is the classification actually wrong? Everything else waits on that.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — find the fatal flaw

The core loop is a lie you're telling yourself. An accountability buddy only works if you respect its judgment — but you're building the judge, and day one it'll be wrong constantly: flag research as slacking, miss that the YouTube video was a tutorial, nag mid-flow. Accountability tools die from one false accusation. (1) Neither is a wedge — "tough love" is a retention feature, not acquisition; nobody installs a screen-watcher that insults them cold. Lead with the tracker. (2) Per-item local-vs-VPS is a trap — nobody hand-classifies their own surveillance. Go 100% local. A VPS holding NSFW/gaming logs is a breach headline. (3) Hermes is over-engineering, full stop. (4) Smallest slice: local category logger → one honest weekly number. No character, no cloud, no agent. Prove the mirror is accurate before you make it talk back.

The First Principles Thinker — what are we really solving?

What are you actually validating? Not "can I track screens" — commodity. You're validating ONE thing: will a person tolerate and come back to a character that insults them using their own honestly-logged data? Only defensible hypothesis. The bet is emotional, not analytical. (1) Wedge = accountability; the call-out IS the product. (2) Storage split premature — ship 100% local; a VPS liability invented before you have a user. (3) Hermes over-engineering badly — self-improvement matters at month 6, not week 1. (4) Smallest slice: local sampling → category → one scheduled personality-loaded call-out per day via the character. Watch whether YOU keep it running two weeks without muting. Build the risky 5% first.

The Expansionist — the upside everyone's missing

Building on Hermes isn't over-engineering — it's the moat everyone skips. A self-improving buddy that learns YOUR patterns becomes non-replaceable; by month three it knows you slack Tuesdays at 3pm and pre-empts it. Near-zero churn. Accountability is the wedge — a roasting character is a personality product that goes viral; the recording of it calling you a bitch IS the marketing. Duolingo-owl energy. The honest log of games/video/NSFW is a goldmine — the most complete self-knowledge dataset a person will own. Sell weekly reality reports; later opt-in cohort benchmarking. Smallest slice: skip the dashboard, ship screen-watch → one daily roast + one number. Make privacy the brand: "your shame stays on your machine." Character franchise, not app.

The Outsider — fresh eyes, zero context

The accountability angle is the only thing I'd remember — I can picture telling a friend. "Productivity tracker" already exists in ten forms. Lead with the buddy. But the swearing would make me not install it on a work laptop where a coworker might hear — let me pick the tone. What stops me cold: it watches my screen, logs "NSFW included," and the brain runs on a "cheap cloud VPS." Cheap and my nudes do not belong in the same sentence. "Per-item choice" doesn't help — I have to trust you got that switch right forever. Default everything local; make cloud a rare obvious opt-in. Say plainly "your screen never leaves your computer unless you flip this." Hermes jargon means nothing — over-engineering. Smallest test: skip cloud, skip brain — a local character that watches one hour then says one honest funny callout.

The Executor — what do you do Monday morning?

Accountability is the wedge. "How productive this week" is a crowded graveyard. A character that roasts you for opening YouTube is the thing people screenshot and share. But you can't build the roast without the tracker underneath — the accountability layer is a thin, loud UI on top of the same data pipeline. Build the pipe, bolt on attitude. Kill Hermes for the MVP — a research toy you'll debug three weeks instead of shipping. This week: (1) background script screenshots every 30–60s, (2) each frame to one cheap vision call → category JSON, (3) write {timestamp,category,app} to local SQLite — nothing leaves the machine, dodge privacy for v0, (4) sum 7 days, print "You were productive 22h. You spent 9h on YouTube." If that sentence makes you flinch, the product works. Storage split and Electron next sprint.

Peer-review highlights — what the reviewers converged on

Strongest response: 4 of 5 reviewers picked the Contrarian for attacking the load-bearing assumption (the judge must be accurate before it can be mean); 1 picked the First Principles Thinker for naming the real hypothesis.

Biggest blind spot: unanimous — the Expansionist, for treating NSFW logs as a monetizable "goldmine" while promising "your shame stays on your machine," and betting the whole moat on an unvalidated accuracy layer.

What ALL five missed: (1) classification accuracy as the core, unsized technical risk; (2) a tough-love buddy is more fragile to misclassification than a neutral dashboard; (3) no definition of "productive" / no ground truth; (4) consent & legality of always-on screen capture (third parties, work laptops, ToS, app-store rejection); (5) per-frame vision cost and week-two retention economics.
